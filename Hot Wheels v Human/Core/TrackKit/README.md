# Core/TrackKit/ — blueprint → validated layout → 3D track (Phase 1, built)

Files (as built — geometry facts in PieceCatalog.swift's header comment)
- `PieceType.swift` — enum: `startGate, finishGate, straight, curve90L, curve90R, curveLarge, hillUp, hillDown, bump, loop, rampJump, portalIn, portalOut`. Raw values are wire format. Portals travel as a back-to-back pair; `SegmentSpec.portalX/Z` (additive optionals — the ONE position on the wire) say where the exit ring sits, the solver teleports the chain there, `LaneSplines.teleports` marks the gap, and the rail follower crosses it instantly (chaos mode doesn't understand portals — rail-pinned is the shipping mode). Down means DOWN: the start gate sits at ground level and hillDowns dig UNDERGROUND (negative levels; no lift-normalisation any more) — supports only stack for levels > 0, the arena mounds a hill over buried pieces, and the chaos fall check is lane-relative.
- `PieceCatalog.swift` — `TrackPieceDefinition` table. Kenney pieces are **not** grid-uniform, so each definition carries measured real-valued offsets in a "traversal frame" (entry at origin, travel +Z, surface y = 0): `exitOffset`, `headingChange`, nominal `FootprintRect`, and a `CenterlineShape` (line/arc/profiled/crest/verticalLoop) for spline generation. Gates = straight + gate-arch overlay model. `curve90L` and `hillDown` reuse the R/up models traversed in reverse.
  - **Hills are role-dependent.** A hill's geometry depends on its neighbours, so `TrackLayoutSolver` resolves each *run* of consecutive same-direction hills and calls `definition(for:role:)` (`HillRole.solo/entry/middle/exit`) — the dictionary entry is the `.solo` form. A lone hill stays the one-piece `hill-complete` (S-curve, level at both ends, +1 level over 0.8 m). A run lays out like real track: `hill-beginning` bends into the slope, plain `track-wide-straight` pitched by `RaceTuning.hillRunSlope` (30°) rides down the middle at +2 levels per 0.8·cos30 m of advance, and `hill-end` flattens out. Chaining `hill-complete` instead gave a staircase of humps, because it returns to level at every seam.
  - `.profiled` carries the mesh's **measured** bed as normalised (t, u) samples. Hills used a straight `.line` chord across an S-curved bed, which floated cars ~26 mm over it and sank them ~29 mm through it. Hill meshes also have a recessed connector tongue, so their bed lift is 0.18 m, not the flat pieces' 0.19 m.
- `BlueprintValidator.swift` — non-empty; ≤ 40 pieces; exactly one `startGate` at index 0; at most one `finishGate`, last (sprint) OR exit meets start (circuit); no negative elevation; footprint overlap per elevation level via axis-aligned world rects (headings are always 90° multiples), skipping the loop because it's an overpass — it advances 0 m, so the straights it joins always sit under its plate and its arc. Kid-readable reasons. Uses the solver's placements so the two can never disagree.
- `TrackLayoutSolver.swift` — accumulates (position, yaw, level) per segment → `PlacedPiece[]` + `LaneSplines` (center/left/right + per-waypoint unit `laterals`, ~0.1 m spacing, lane offsets per `RaceTuning.laneOffsetWide`, tapered to center through the narrow loop). `laterals` is the track frame's "left" vector — the rail follower derives its up vector from `cross(tangent, lateral)`, which is what caught the long-standing mirrored z-sign in the arc laterals (the "left" lane used to sweep across to the right through every curve and jog back at the exit seam). Pure math, no RealityKit.
- `TrackSpawner.swift` — RealityKit: loads models via `AssetStore`, places them, `generateStaticMesh` collision per model part (convex hulls would seal the loop), tags gate overlays with `CheckpointComponent`. Cars collide with SOLVED geometry, not the visual meshes: flat pieces get one bed slab (pitched along their rise), and anything whose bed a slab can't describe — the loop's ring and the `.profiled` hills, whose measured bed bows up to 7 cm off its own chord — gets `splineCollision`, a chain of boxes riding the piece's own centreline with `cross(tangent, lateral)` for up (the same track frame the rail follower rolls cars with). Only the jump ramp keeps its exact mesh. Cosmetic `supports*` legs under elevated pieces: the models are authored exactly one elevation level tall (0.2 m) with the base at the origin, so the stack runs one leg per level at the footprint centre, from whatever is underneath up to the bed. `deckLevel(atX:z:under:in:)` finds that "underneath" — where track crosses over track, the stack starts on the lower deck's level rather than at the ground, because a ground-up stack stands its legs ON the orange track and runs through it. Levels are exact, so the top of the stack MEETS the bed it holds up; only the ground-planted bottom leg stretches (`legPlant`) down into the play mat. Sinking the whole stack instead, as it used to, left 4 cm of daylight under the track. Narrow-model pieces (the loop) get `supports`, the rest `supports-wide`. Legs are collision-free (a car flying off must fall past them) and hills are skipped — their bed slopes through the piece, so a full-height post would pierce a `hillDown`, and the flat neighbours at each end carry the legs.
- `AssetStore.swift` — `@MainActor` class (not an actor: Entity is MainActor-bound), async `entity(named:)`, cache + clone-on-vend.
- `RandomTrackGenerator.swift` — NOT built yet; arrives with the "shuffle" button in Phase 5 (BUILD-ORDER).
