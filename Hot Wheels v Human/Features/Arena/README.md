# Features/Arena/ — the 3D race scene (Phases 1–2 solo, 3 on TV)

Files
- `ArenaView.swift` — the `RealityView`: builds scene from `AppModel` blueprint via `TrackSpawner`, spawns cars via `CarFactory`, registers `DriveSystem`/`RaceRulesSystem`, directional light, inline chase camera (see below). Hosts overlay HUD. The `RealityView` alone takes `.ignoresSafeArea()` — scene edge to edge (under the iPad status bar, out to the TV's overscan), HUD and PiPs inside the safe area.
- `ArenaEnvironment.swift` (moved to `Core/RaceCore/` — no SwiftUI, and RaceSession now builds the world itself so it's ready BEFORE the countdown; the async re-theme here lagged behind it and races opened on the lobby's green field) — the world around the track: procedural sky dome (gradient + puffy clouds on candy/day/sunset, stars on space — both drawn chunky because the chase cam only ever samples a thin band of a texture that wraps 360°; clouds are redrawn a texture-width to each side so the seam can't slice them) + 90 m checkered play-mat ground + scattered toy props (item-* USDZ), themed per trackId (candy/day/sunset/space — order is load-bearing, byte-sum % `hashedThemeCount`, which stays 4: worlds appended after those are pick-only, so adding one can't re-theme existing tracks). **Pickable worlds** (Big City / Hometown / Park, Kenney city+nature kits at 0.3 scale) ride `TrackBlueprint.worldTheme` (additive optional on the wire) → `RaceSession.worldTheme` → `make(for:theme:around:)`; `structured` themes snap props to a 0.7 m street grid, one per cell, yaw squared toward the track — random-yaw buildings read as knocked over. Rebuilt per race via `RaceSession.trackID`/`trackFootprint`/`worldTheme`/`scenery`; props stay out of the track's footprint and carry no collision. The ground is a low-poly **terrain heightfield** (flat under the track + prop ring, rolling hills beyond, a mountain rim at the world's edge; the physics floor stays a flat invisible box), each theme rings **horizon silhouettes** (`Theme.horizon`, ~14 oversized clones at radius 31–40 m), and `RaceTuning.groundlessThemes` (space) skips the visual ground entirely — the track floats, `RaceSession` strips the `support-*` legs, and horizon props float at varied heights. Hand-placed `SceneryItem`s from the builder spawn via `SceneryPlacer` (Core/TrackKit). **The world moves**: `AmbientMotionComponent/System` (coins spin, ships/spacecraft/ghosts bob+roll, flags and trees sway — assigned by model name via `AmbientMotion`, applied to scatter, horizon, and hand-placed props alike), `WalkerSystem` strolls placed people, `TrainSystem` chugs Winter's 3-car toy train on an ellipse just outside the prop ring. City/Hometown/Speedway use `Theme.cityBlocks`: an AUTOTILED street grid (`streetTile(north:south:east:west:)` — crossroad/tee/bend/straight/dead-end chosen from neighbour connectivity, so roads read as roads even where the track punches through), buildings filling the blocks facing their street (600-entity cap for runaway tracks). `Theme.traffic` cars drive the grid via `TrafficSystem`: right-lane offset, stop-sign pause at intersections, braking for `WalkerComponent` pedestrians ahead, and a fade-out at dead ends after which the SAME car re-enters at a road START (a degree-1 cell, driving inward — `TrafficSystem.roadStart`). Placed vehicles (`SceneryPlacer.vehicles`, the Cars shelf) join traffic too: off-road they bee-line for the nearest connected cell (`seeking`), then drive the union of the world's auto grid and hand-laid `street-*` tiles (`handStreetCells`, 0.7 m snap); with no roads at all they wander leashed lazy curves (`WanderComponent`). City worlds also spawn **sidewalk strollers**: `PedestrianSystem` walks people along the road edges (street grid resampled at 0.35 m, `sidewalkCells`, ± side offset) joined with any hand-laid pavement into ONE network; each step past a building carries a 1-in-100 shop visit (walk to the door, fade in, browse, fade back out — `doorBeside` only fires on real building cells, auto blocks + hand-placed city buildings). Hand-placed people near any sidewalk join the same network with the same doors; cars brake for them too. Barrier roads (`street-barrier-*`) turn NPC traffic around instead of fading it. **Underground**: terrain mounds a hill over every buried track piece (`TunnelMound`, cosine dome, terrain mesh at 120 segments), and `GroundFadeSystem` fades the terrain to 30% whenever any car is below it — the x-ray view that keeps a tunnelling car visible. Placeable sky-stuff (`SpaceStuff` in SceneryPlacer): procedural planets/moon/nebulae — no kit has them — floating at toy-sky height, slowly turning; nebulae are billboarded gradient quads. `TrackBlueprint.worldEmpty` skips ALL auto-placed stuff, keeping sky/terrain — the blank canvas option.
- `ArenaHUDView.swift` — TV overlay: countdown numerals, per-player name+lives banners, lap counter, finish banner, results panel (times, crashes, best segment, REMATCH prompt "press READY on iPad").
- Chase camera — inline in `ArenaView`'s update subscription (no separate `CameraSystem.swift`): follows the cars' midpoint, pulls back proportional to their separation, smooth lerp. **Loop swing:** a loop's circle lies in the plane of travel, so from straight behind it's edge-on and reads as a wall — while a car is on or nearing a loop (detected via `LaneFollowComponent.loopRanges`, lead-in `RaceTuning.loopCamLead`) the camera eases to a 3/4 side angle (`loopBias`) so the ring reads as a circle. Finish/crash special cams are not built yet. Solo Arena on iPad reuses everything.
  **Unblocking:** elevated track parks itself between the camera and the cars, and from behind a deck the race is an orange wall. Every frame a `Scene.raycast` from the DEFAULT camera spot to the hero car asks whether anything that isn't a car is in the way (hits are walked up to a `CarComponent` — the collision that answers is usually an unnamed child of a `bed-N` holder, so names are no use). If it is still blocked `RaceTuning.camBlockedGrace` seconds later, the camera walks `RaceTuning.camEscapes` in order — climb over it, duck under it, round each side, head-on, high-and-wide — and moves to the first spot that can see the car, easing at `camEscapeEase` instead of the lazy framing drift. Testing the DEFAULT spot rather than the current one is what stops the escape undoing itself the moment it works. Measured on a 75-piece random track: no spell of lost view over 5 s (worst 3.8 s), against 33 s before.
- Driver dashboard (`DriverDashboardView`) — a moulded cowl bolted to the bottom edge,
  rides along with driver view and stows in the chase camera (no cockpit to bolt it to).
  OPAQUE, full width, anchored past the safe area: the first pass was a translucent rounded
  strip and it just showed sky through it, reading as a floating toolbar rather than a dash.
  Carries a speed gauge (real telemetry off the hero racer), the car radio (with the music
  volume knob beside its display window — inside the faceplate, because Solo Arena's boost
  dial sits over the speaker corner where a real radio would keep it; tvOS has no `Slider`,
  so `#if os(iOS)`), and a punched
  speaker grille that stops short of the bottom-trailing corner so Solo Arena's boost dial
  lands on bare moulding instead of on the speaker. A `ViewThatFits` sheds the trim before
  the controls on narrow screens — speaker first, then the gauge — because portrait iPad is
  834 pt and the full row wants ~800.
- Every car gets its own dash (`DashStyle`, built from the hero's `CarDesign`). Chassis picks
  the material: heavy muscle is brushed steel with exposed bolts, balanced formula is carbon
  weave, superlight drift is dark plastic with a lit underline. The paint colours everything
  that glows — display, gauge numerals, lit keys, power key — lifted toward legibility first
  (`Color.dashAccent`), since a navy car would otherwise get a navy display nobody can read
  from the couch, and black paint comes back chrome. Finish sets the top lip's gloss (matte
  barely catches the sky, glossy flares) and sparkle flecks the moulding. The car's name is
  stamped on the gauge housing like a real dash badge.
  The radio: a lit display window, a round power key, and six numbered preset keys
  (`RadioStation`) built to look pressable — domed gradient, hard top highlight, amber when
  lit. One tap swaps the music on the spot via `SoundBank.playMusic`, and the pick sticks on
  `RaceCoordinator.radioStation`, so the next race starts on that station instead of always
  `race_intensity`. Power off (`radioOn`) means no race music either. Every key draws its own
  focus ring off `\.isFocused` — `.buttonStyle(.plain)` gives the Siri Remote nothing to
  show otherwise. The HUD's race clock takes a `bottomInset` to sit above the dash.
- At the flag (`phase == .results`) the camera leaves the cockpit no matter what the toggle
  says, closes in (`RaceTuning.finishCamZoom`) and aims BESIDE the pack
  (`finishCamSideBias`) so the cars slide into two thirds of the frame and the results panel
  docks in the last third instead of sitting on top of them — the loser coming apart at the
  line is the best part of the race. The dashboard stows for the same reason.
- Driver view — the default. The camera is PARENTED to the hero car (first non-AI racer still in the race), not re-aimed at it every frame: a world-space `look()` reads the car's transform one frame stale, and the hood juddered in and out of the near plane. As a child it composes with the car's current pose, so the hood is nailed to the bottom fifth of the frame and a loop rolls the view upside down for free. Local offset: `RaceTuning.driverCamEyeRatio` car-heights up (height recovered from `CarComponent.rideHeight`, which excludes the driver poking over the roof) and `driverCamNoseRatio` car-LENGTHS forward — lengths, because every model is 0.175 m long while heights vary 2:1, and a height-scaled nose put the monster truck's camera past its own bumper. `driverCamPitch` tips it onto the hood; `driverCamNear` is a toy-scale near plane (the 0.01 m default sliced a 5 cm car's hood in half). Wreck the hero and it falls back to the chase camera, which reparents the camera to the scene root. The top-trailing toggle (`RaceCoordinator.driverView`) swaps the two — one button for iPad taps and Siri Remote clicks; covered by `ArenaCameraUITests`.
- **Track switch** — three icon-only buttons under the camera toggle (`RaceCoordinator.trackVisibility`, `TrackVisibility` in TrackKit): eye open = show all track, eye half-closed = hide ghost pieces (the default — a ghost you can see isn't a ghost), eye slashed = hide the lot and watch the cars fly on nothing. No words: it has to read at 3 m from a sofa, and `Label(...).labelStyle(.iconOnly)` still hands the words to VoiceOver. It only sets `OpacityComponent` on the tagged visuals (`TrackVisualComponent`) — a ghost's support legs go with it, collision beds are never touched, so hiding track changes nothing but what you see. Re-applied after every race in the series, since each race spawns a fresh track. The SAME `TrackVisibilityPicker` sits on the iPad controller (`DashboardView`): the host owns the state, an iPad asks for a change with `GameMessage.trackVisibility`, and every snapshot carries the answer back — so whichever end the kid reaches for, both agree. Covered by `GhostTrackUITests` and `trackSwitchStaysInSyncBothWays`.
- `SoloArenaView.swift` — iPad wrapper: full-bleed Arena with `RaceControlsView` floating over it (bottom-trailing, 24 pt inset — clear of the bottom-leading reaction-cam PiP and the bottom-centre race clock), wired via `LoopbackTransport`. Only the *buttons* come over from the dashboard — boost, cam, rematch; progress/lives/speed already live in the HUD's per-racer banner, and the full `DashboardView` column is still what the iPad shows in the Race-on-TV flow where there's no arena on the device. (This is Test Mode's home too — Test Mode = Solo Arena with `MatchConfig.mode == .test`, two local designs, no lives/boost). Also home to the **workshop "try it" kit**, since every use of it is "present a Solo Arena over what I'm building":
  - `.racePreview(isPresented:designs:blueprint:config:)` — full-screen cover with a labelled Close button, fresh race session per presentation (`runID`). Used by TrackBuilder, Customizer, CharacterEditor and TestMode so the gesture — big yellow button, race, X, keep building — is identical everywhere.
  - `TryItButton` / `SaveItButton` — the paired workshop buttons ("See it in 3D!" / "Test Drive!" + "Save it!"). Both `lineLimit(1).fixedSize()`: in a crowded toolbar SwiftUI would rather stack a label into "Se / e it / in / 3D" than overflow, which reads as a broken button.

  Workshops preview what's **on screen**, not what's saved — they pass `AppModel.stampedRaceDesign(car:driver:)` overrides and (for the builder) the live `model.blueprint`. That's the whole point: no save, no backing out.
- `VFX.swift` — boost tailpipe glow (emissive material pulse + particle if cheap), debris despawn fade, confetti burst on win (`ParticleEmitterComponent`).

Perf budget (Apple TV 4K): ≤ 40 track pieces + 2 cars + debris ≤ 150 draw-relevant entities; static track merged where possible; target steady 60 fps. Profile with RealityKit debug options before adding VFX. If PiP reaction cam (2nd RealityView) costs > 10% frame time → sprite fallback per ReactionCam/README.
