# Features/TrackBuilder/ — iPad and Apple TV 3D snap editor

Files
- `TrackBuilderView.swift` — layout: piece palette (bottom scroll), 3D scene up top with an overhead mini-map in the corner (a Button: tap toggles corner-size ↔ reading-size), toolbar. Toolbar = icon-only undo/clear/shuffle + **"Race it!"** + "Save it!". Icon-only is load-bearing, not styling: spelled out, five labelled buttons overflow an iPad in portrait and every one wraps into an unreadable stack (`Label` still hands the words to VoiceOver). "Race it!" races the blueprint *currently on the canvas* via `.racePreview` — no save required, because peeking mid-build is the point; "Save it!" is what persists the track and makes it race next.
- `TrackBuilder3DView.swift` — shared RealityKit canvas with real USDZ pieces and the selected arena world. iPad retains orbit, pinch, pan and ground hit testing. Apple TV reads optional `TVTrackControls` for camera movement, a yellow ground-placement cross and a cyan selected-piece marker. Camera fit still follows the track footprint. Async track/world/decoration rebuilds check their request key after loading, so older work cannot replace a newer edit.
- `TrackBuilderModel.swift` — shared working blueprint and validator-safe append/remove-last track editing. Segment indices stay stable until the last piece or portal pair is removed. Blueprint identity stays stable while previewing and editing; saving makes a new library copy and reports persistence success. Loading restores world/theme/Empty World, portals, ghosts and scenery, and clears any held selection. Decorations have editor-only stable row identities, with move/rotate/remove-selected methods in addition to undo-last. No wire-format changes.
- `TVTrackBuilderView.swift` — remote-native editor with permanent Pieces, Decorate, Worlds, Camera and Library inspectors. The iPad uses the original touch layout. The TV uses the shared workshop button style, persistent selected states, explicit labels, 64 pt or larger controls and separate directional focus sections.
- `TVTrackControls.swift` — observable, testable camera and cursor input. Cursor directions follow the camera, with 0.7 m or 0.35 m steps. Placement covers the complete track footprint plus a 30 m surrounding margin, including exits on long tracks. Camera buttons expose orbit/tilt/pan/zoom/reset; editor input never alters race physics.
- `PiecePaletteView.swift` — cards per `PieceType` (icon + name); cards gray out when appending them would fail validation (e.g. overlap) — try-validate on the fly. The row LEADS with the **Ghost** card (`circle.dashed`, `ghostToggle`): a sticky mode, not a one-shot, so a whole invisible stretch is one tap here then normal piece taps. Pieces appended while it's lit get `SegmentSpec.isGhost` and come out see-through in the builder (30%, `TrackSpawner.setOpacity`) and dashed on the mini-map — invisible only once the arena hides them. Ghosts are visuals-only: the bed, the spline and the checkpoints are identical, so the car drives the piece it can't see. **Tap a piece already on the track to flip it** (`toggleGhost(at:)`) — ghost mode is for laying a run of them, the tap is for changing your mind about one, which is most of what building actually is. The tap hit-tests the track's collision (`TrackSpawner.makeTappable`/`pieceIndex(of:)`), so it picks the right piece at any elevation and a see-through ghost is exactly as tappable as a solid one.
- `TrackCanvasView.swift` — the overhead schematic (mini-map + preset/saved thumbnails): colored segments per piece type, lane centerline, piece badges in full-size mode. Auto-fits the layout; no pan/zoom of its own — zoom = the mini-map size toggle.
- Blueprint network submission belongs to `Features/Dashboard/DashboardModel.swift`, which sends the selected ranked tracks through the existing local transport. This folder does not create another networking path.

## Apple TV interaction parity

| iPad action | Apple TV remote action |
| --- | --- |
| Tap a piece in the shelf | Select its Pieces button; invalid appends are disabled |
| Tap a placed track piece | Select its numbered row and Make ghost / Make solid |
| Pick a portal and tap the ground | Pick Portal, move the yellow target and Place here; overlap keeps the current exit and offers another try |
| Tap a placed portal exit | Select its row and Move portal exit |
| Pick a decoration and tap the ground | Pick its thumbnail, move the target and Place here |
| Tap a prop to pick up, move or spin | Pick its placed-item row, move the target or Rotate quarter turn; Remove selected is explicit |
| Drag, pinch or pan the canvas | Camera inspector buttons; Fit track restores the original framing |
| Select world / Empty World | Worlds inspector, with persistent yellow selection |
| Expand the mini-map | Enlarge map / Shrink map button |
| Load a starter or saved track | Library, available during every build rather than only on a fresh canvas |
| Save / scrap a saved track | Name the next copy, Save a copy; explicit Remove with confirmation |
| Undo / clear / shuffle / preview race | Permanent footer; clear confirms and preserves the library |

The target can move through individual buttons or a dedicated remote input mode.
In remote input mode, directions move the target, Select places it and Back
returns to the inspector. Navigation elsewhere keeps the normal tvOS focus
behavior. Decoration selection opens a focused placement inspector so users do
not have to navigate past every prop to reach the placement controls. Both
platforms share street/path grid snapping, portal validation, scenery limits,
local persistence and unsaved race previews. Track pieces keep their derived
orientation on both platforms; free track rotation is not an iPad capability.

`Hot Wheels v HumanTests/TVTrackBuilderTests.swift` adds eight focused checks for
identity, restored settings, selected-decoration deletion, road snapping,
non-finite portal rejection, camera-relative cursor motion, long-track reach,
and camera bounds/reset. Runtime focus, placement and preview parity still need
the app's coordinated tvOS Simulator or hardware verification before release.

**Decorate mode** (the paintbrush toggle): the palette becomes the decoration box — every world's props (`DecorPaletteView`, world filter row + thumbnail row; thumbnails are pre-rendered PNGs in `Resources/Thumbs`, loaded by explicit bundle URL because named-image lookup missed loose files). Extra shelves: **Streets** (road tiles snap to the FULL 0.7 m traffic grid so hand-laid roads join the street graph; paths snap to a half tile; both place unrotated so they butt cleanly), **Cars** (16 vehicles incl. firetruck/garbage truck/tractor; placed vehicles drive: they seek the nearest road — auto grid or hand-laid — join traffic, and respawn at road starts; NO roads → they wander lazy leashed curves near the placement spot, `WanderComponent`). People placed near ANY sidewalk — hand-laid pavement (`street-square` / `city-path-*`) or a city world's road edges — walk one joined 0.35 m network (`PedestrianComponent`; road cells add a ±0.26 side offset that fades out onto hand pavement), turn around at ends instead of fading, and share the city strollers' 1-in-100 shop-visit door list (auto blocks + hand-placed `city-house/shop/skyscraper`, `SceneryPlacer.handBuildingCells`); far from pavement they keep the little patrol and **People** (mini-characters with a baked walk clip; they patrol a little line from where they're placed — `WalkerComponent`). **Barrier roads** (`street-barrier-a/b`, composed dead-end tile + roadblock): placeable NPC roads where traffic TURNS AROUND instead of fading out (`TrafficComponent.barriers`). The piece shelf ends with **Portal**: one tap appends the ring PAIR (`portalIn` + `portalOut`), then the next ground tap places the exit ring (`placePortalExit`, validator-guarded; undo removes the pair). Tracks may DIG: hillDown below ground level is legal, and while the track has buried pieces the builder forces the terrain to ~30% opacity (`GroundFadeComponent.forced`) so the kid never fights the dirt. Tap a prop → tap the ground to place (`SceneryItem` on the blueprint, capped at `RaceTuning.maxSceneryItems`); tap a placed prop to pick it up (it lifts + scales) → tap to set down, or tap the HELD prop again to spin it a quarter turn; Undo removes the last placement. While decorating, one-finger drag PANS across the ground (orbit is build-mode's gesture) — you have to reach past the track to build a town. The world strip's **Empty** card (`TrackBlueprint.worldEmpty`, additive optional) keeps the theme's sky/terrain but none of the auto-placed stuff. The ground tap point comes from `TrackBuilder3DView.groundPoint` — our own frustum cast through `cameraPose` (iOS tap gestures are 2D; the targeted gesture only says WHICH entity was hit). Placed props are tap targets only in the builder (`SceneryPlacer.spawn(tappable:)`) — the arena spawns them collision-free.

UX guardrails (kid-first): no free placement/rotation for TRACK pieces — they always attach to the open exit with auto-derived orientation (this is why the builder is simple AND why the piece list has no rotation field). Decorations are the free-placement outlet, and they're cosmetic so nothing can break. Difficulty meter = count of loop/bump/ramp pieces as `flame.fill` SF Symbols (never emoji — CLAUDE.md). Empty state shows a ghost "start gate goes here" hint.
